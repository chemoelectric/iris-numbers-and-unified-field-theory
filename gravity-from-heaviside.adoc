Newton’s inverse-square law of gravitation emerges directly in
Heaviside’s 3-vector calculus, from his form of Maxwell’s equations.
One can combine Maxwell’s stress-energy conservation law with the
static, spherically symmetric field of a localized energy/mass
distribution, or one can equivalently use Heaviside’s 1893
gravitational field equations. These mirror his electromagnetic
equations.

== Heaviside’s Maxwell Equations and the Field Stress-Energy Tensor

Start with Oliver Heaviside’s four vector equations for regions of low
external interference,footnote:[Commonly but misleadingly called “free
space.”] with source charge density \(\rho \) and current density
\(\mathbf{J}\):

. \(\nabla \cdot \mathbf{E} = \frac{\rho}{\varepsilon_0}\)

. \(\nabla \cdot \mathbf{B} = 0\)

. \(\nabla \times \mathbf{E} = -\frac{\partial \mathbf{B}}{\partial t}\)

. \(\nabla \times \mathbf{B} = \mu_0 \mathbf{J} + \mu_0 \varepsilon_0 \frac{\partial \mathbf{E}}{\partial t}\)

Taking the dot product of equation (3) with
\(\frac{1}{\mu_0}\mathbf{B}\) and equation (4) with \(\varepsilon_0
\mathbf{E}\), subtracting, and applying the standard Heaviside vector
identity \(\nabla \cdot (\mathbf{E} \times \mathbf{B}) = \mathbf{B}
\cdot (\nabla \times \mathbf{E}) - \mathbf{E} \cdot (\nabla \times
\mathbf{B})\) yields Poynting’s energy conservation theorem:
\(\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}+\nabla \cdot
\mathbf{S}=-\mathbf{J}\cdot \mathbf{E}\) where the electromagnetic
field energy density is \(u=\frac{1}{2}\left(\varepsilon
_{0}|{}\mathbf{E}|{}^{2}+\frac{1}{\mu
_{0}}|{}\mathbf{B}|{}^{2}\right)\) and the energy flux (Poynting
vector) is \(\mathbf{S} = \frac{1}{\mu_0}(\mathbf{E} \times
\mathbf{B})\).

== Deriving Newton’s Inverse-Square Law from Maxwellian Energy Density and Stress Balance

To obtain static gravity directly from Maxwell’s electromagnetic field
without introducing any non-Maxwellian postulates:

. __Equate Inertial/Gravitational Mass Density to Electromagnetic Field Energy Density:__
From Maxwell’s radiation pressure relation \(p = u/c^2\) (where \(c^2
{empty}= 1/(\mu_0 \varepsilon_0)\)), any localized electromagnetic
field configuration of total internal energy \(U = \int_V u \, dV\)
carries an equivalent inertial/gravitational mass:
\(M=\frac{U}{c^{2}}=\mu _{0}\varepsilon _{0}\int _{V}u\,dV,\ \rho
_{m}(\mathbf{r})=\frac{u(\mathbf{r})}{c^{2}}\)

. Maxwell Stress Tensor Divergence in Static Equilibrium:
Using Heaviside’s vector identity \( ( \nabla \times \mathbf{E} ) \times
\mathbf{E} = ( \mathbf{E} \cdot \nabla )\mathbf{E} -
\frac{1}{2}\nabla ( \vert{}\mathbf{E}\vert{} ^ 2 )\) (and the identical
identity for \(\mathbf{B}\)), the electromagnetic Lorentz force
density \(\mathbf{f} = \rho \mathbf{E} + \mathbf{J} \times
\mathbf{B}\) rewrites purely in terms of field gradients as the
divergence of Maxwell’s stress tensor \(\mathbf{T}\):
\(\mathbf{f}=\nabla \cdot \mathbf{T}-\varepsilon _{0}\mu
_{0}\frac{\partial \mathbf{S}}{\partial t}\). In a static configuration
(\(\partial_t = 0\)), the radial stress across a concentric spherical
shell of radius \(r\) enclosing total field energy \(U = M c^2\)
scales inversely with the surface area \(4\pi r^2\) of the enclosing
sphere.

. __Medium Constitutive Perturbation (\(\varepsilon, \mu\)) and Gravitational Acceleration:__
In a pervasive electromagnetic medium, a localized concentration of
field energy density \(\rho_m = u/c^2\) perturbs the surrounding
vacuum permittivity and permeability (\(\varepsilon(r) = \varepsilon_0
n(r)\), \(\mu(r) = \mu_0 n(r)\)) so the medium satisfies
Poisson’s static energy-coupling equation: \(\nabla \cdot
\mathbf{g}=-4\pi G\rho _{m}=-\frac{4\pi G}{c^{2}}u\) where
\(\mathbf{g} = -\nabla \Phi\) is the radial inward acceleration field
produced by the constitutive gradient \(-\frac{c^2}{2}\nabla n(r)\),
and \(G\) is the medium’s elastic-electromagnetic coupling constant.

. __Integrating Over a Sphere of Radius \(r\) via Gauss’s Divergence Theorem:__
Integrate \( \nabla \cdot \mathbf{g} = -\frac{4\pi G}{c ^ 2} u\) over a
sphere \(V\) of radius \(r\) enclosing total electromagnetic energy
\(U = M c ^ 2\): \(\int _ {V}(\nabla \cdot \mathbf{g})\,dV=-\frac{4\pi
G}{c ^ {2}}\int _ {V}u\,dV=-4\pi GM\) Applying Gauss’s divergence theorem
\(\int_V (\nabla \cdot \mathbf{g})\, dV = \oint _ {\partial V}
\mathbf{g} \cdot d\mathbf{A}\) on the spherical surface of area \(4\pi
r ^ 2\) (with \(\mathbf{g} = g(r)\hat{\mathbf{r}}\) by spherical
symmetry): \(g(r)\,(4\pi r ^ {2})=-4\pi GM\implies
\mathbf{g}(r)=-\frac{GM}{r ^ {2}}\hat{\mathbf{r}}\) For a second localized
energy concentration \(m = U_{\text{test}}/c ^ 2\) placed at distance
\(r\), the net Maxwell stress imbalance across its volume gives
Newton’s law of universal gravitation:
\(\mathbf{F}=m\mathbf{g}(r)=-\frac{GMm}{r ^ {2}}\hat{\mathbf{r}}\)

== Heaviside’s Direct Vector Gravity Equations (1893)

In his 1893 paper "A Gravitational and Electromagnetic Analogy" (The
Electrician, Vol. 31), Oliver Heaviside also wrote down the four
vector field equations for gravity directly in his own vector notation
by replacing \((\mathbf{E}, \mathbf{B}, \rho, \varepsilon_0, \mu_0)\)
with \((\mathbf{g}, \mathbf{h}, \rho_m, -\frac{1}{4\pi G}, -\frac{4\pi
G}{c ^ 2})\):

. \(\nabla \cdot \mathbf{g} = -4\pi G \rho_m\)

. \(\nabla \cdot \mathbf{h} = 0\)

. \(\nabla \times \mathbf{g} = -\frac{\partial \mathbf{h}}{\partial t}\)

. \(\nabla \times \mathbf{h} = -\frac{4\pi G}{c ^ 2}\mathbf{J}_m + \frac{1}{c ^ 2}\frac{\partial \mathbf{g}}{\partial t}\)

Setting \(\partial_t = 0\) and \(\mathbf{J}_m = 0\) reduces equations
(1) and (3) immediately to \(\nabla \times \mathbf{g} = 0\)
(\(\mathbf{g} = -\nabla \Phi\)) and \(\nabla \cdot \mathbf{g} = -4\pi
G \rho_m\), which integrates over a sphere of radius \(r\) to
\(\mathbf{g} = -\frac{G M}{r ^ 2}\hat{\mathbf{r}}\).
